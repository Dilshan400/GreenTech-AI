# University Academics' Perspectives on Purchasing Green Smartphones in Sri Lanka

**Authors**: Pivithuru Janak Kumarasinghe (Zhejiang Shuren University / Univ. of Sri Jayewardenepura), Qiubo Huang (Zhejiang Shuren University), & Nilmini Rathnayake (SLIIT Business School, Sri Lanka)  
**Publication**: *Interdisciplinary Journal of Information, Knowledge, and Management (IJIKM)*, Vol. 18, 2023, pp. 369–385.

---

### Research Background: The Smartphone Proliferation & E-Waste Crisis
* **Proliferation**: In Sri Lanka, digital literacy reached $49.5\%$ with $89\%$ of internet users accessing online platforms primarily through mobile devices.
* **Environmental Impact**:
  - Approximately **$80\%$ of a smartphone’s lifetime carbon footprint occurs during manufacturing**, $16\%$ during consumption, and $4\%$ during logistics/shipping.
  - A single smartphone contains over **62 distinct metallic elements** (including rare earth metals and heavy toxins like lead and mercury).
  - Globally, out of $7.3\text{ kg}$ of e-waste generated per capita, only $1.7\text{ kg}$ is recycled in an environmentally sound manner.
* **Green Smartphone Definition**: Devices engineered with modular replaceable components, conflict-free minerals, non-toxic materials, high energy efficiency, and extended physical lifespans (e.g., Fairphone).

---

### Core Theoretical Themes Examined
Using in-depth qualitative semi-structured interviews with university faculty members across Applied Sciences, Engineering, Business, Medicine, and Agriculture in Sri Lanka:
1. **Green Awareness (GA)**: Knowledge of smartphone life-cycle impacts, radiation emission hazards, and energy footprints.
2. **Environmental Concern (EC)**: Recognition that rapid upgrade cycles and planned obsolescence aggravate local electronic waste dumps.
3. **Altruism (AL)**: Personal readiness to make financial and convenience sacrifices for the ecological well-being of future generations.
4. **Willingness to Pay (WTP)**: Preparedness to pay a monetary premium for eco-friendly certified mobile phones.

---

### Key Empirical Findings
* **Strong Altruistic Motivation**: Academics demonstrated high altruism, viewing eco-friendly smartphone purchases not just as personal gadgets, but as moral commitments to curb e-waste for future generations.
* **Positive Willingness to Pay Premium**: Participants explicitly stated that paying higher prices for green smartphones is justified provided there is transparent proof of recycled materials, energy footprint reductions, and fair labor practices.
* **Willingness to Resist Rapid Upgrades**: Academics criticized constant annual upgrade cycles driven by manufacturers and expressed a strong preference for durable, easily repairable modular handsets.

---

### Implications for Green Electronic Systems
* **Targeting Opinion Leaders**: University academics and educators act as key opinion leaders who can champion green technology choices among students.
* **Educational Transparency**: Device specifications presented to consumers should highlight modularity, repairability indices, and recycled material percentages alongside traditional camera/processor specs.
