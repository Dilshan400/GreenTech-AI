# Factors Influencing Green Purchase Intention: A Comprehensive Meta-Analysis of 54 Empirical Studies

**Authors**: Wencan Zhuang, Xiaoguang Luo, & Muhammad Usman Riaz (School of Economics and Management, Harbin University of Science and Technology, China)  
**Publication**: *Frontiers in Psychology*, Vol. 12, Article 644020, April 2021, pp. 1–15.

---

### Research Background & Significance
Due to conflicting empirical findings across global green marketing literature (e.g., whether knowledge, subjective norms, or attitudes consistently predict green buying), this paper conducts a quantitative **meta-analysis of 54 empirical studies (covering over 13,000 respondents)** published between 2010 and 2020 using Comprehensive Meta-Analysis (CMA 3.0) software.

---

### Classification of Factors
The study integrates the **Theory of Planned Behavior (TPB)** and the **Attitude-Behavior-Context (ABC)** model into three overarching categories:
1. **Cognitive Factors**: Green Perceived Value (GPV), Green Perceived Quality (GPQ), Green Perceived Risk (GPR), Perceived Behavioral Control (PBC), Perceived Consumer Effectiveness (PCE), and Environmental Knowledge (EK).
2. **Individual Characteristics**: Consumer Attitude (ATT), Green Trust (GT), and Environmental Concern (EC).
3. **Social Factors**: Subjective Norms (SN) and Collectivism (COL).

---

### Core Theoretical & Practical Implications
1. **Perceived Value and Trust Drive Buying Intentions**: Green perceived value ($r = 0.596$), positive attitude ($r = 0.535$), and green trust ($r = 0.530$) are the triad of decisive drivers. Consumers must perceive tangible utility, believe product sustainability claims, and feel that the benefits justify the costs.
2. **The Barrier of Perceived Risk**: Green perceived risk has a significant negative coefficient ($r = -0.373$). When consumers fear greenwashing or doubt product reliability/longevity, purchase intention drops steeply.
3. **Implications for Green Tech Systems**:
   - Provide verified, reliable certifications (e.g., EPEAT, Energy Star, RoHS) to systematically minimize green perceived risk.
   - Emphasize clear lifetime cost savings and durability metrics to maximize green perceived value.
