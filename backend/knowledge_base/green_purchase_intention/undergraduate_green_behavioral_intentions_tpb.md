# Impact of Green Factors on Undergraduate Students' Green Behavioral Intentions: A Hybrid Two-Stage Modeling Approach

**Author**: Sanjoy Kumar Roy (General Education Department, City University, Dhaka, Bangladesh)  
**Publication**: *Heliyon* (Cell Press / Elsevier), Vol. 9, Issue 10, October 2023, Article e20630.

---

### Research Context & Rationale
Undergraduate university students represent the future consumer base and critical decision-makers for sustainable consumption. This study investigates the behavioral drivers and necessary conditions that determine undergraduate students' **Green Behavioral Intentions (GBI)** using an expanded **Theory of Planned Behavior (TPB)** combined with Partial Least Squares Structural Equation Modeling (PLS-SEM) and Necessary Condition Analysis (NCA).

---

### Core Theoretical Constructs
1. **Green TPB Perception (GTP)**: A higher-order reflective-formative construct combining:
   - Green Personal Attitude (GPA): Favorable evaluation of green purchases.
   - Green Subjective Norms (GSN): Peer, family, and faculty social expectations.
   - Green Behavioral Control (GBC): Confidence and perceived ease of buying green.
2. **Green Price Sensitivity (GPS)**: Responsiveness to price adjustments and willingness to spend on sustainable alternatives.
3. **Green Product Trust (GPT)**: Consumer conviction regarding reliability, dependability, and authentic ecological claims.
4. **Green Product Value (GPV)**: Overall assessment of net environmental and functional benefits versus expenditure.
5. **Green Environmental Awareness (GEA - Moderator)**: Comprehension of environmental degradation, pollution, and personal conservation responsibility.

---

### Key Empirical Findings
Empirical assessment based on **382 valid undergraduate student responses**:

#### 1. High Explanatory Power:
The structural model explained **$84.4\%$ of the variance in Green Behavioral Intentions** ($R^2 = 0.844$, $\text{SRMR} = 0.038$).

#### 2. Direct Hypotheses (PLS-SEM Path Analysis):
| Hypothesis & Path | Path Coefficient ($\beta$) | $t$-statistic | $p$-value | Empirical Conclusion |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **H1: GTP $\rightarrow$ GBI** | **$0.420$** | **$7.551$** | **$p < 0.001$** | **Supported (Strongest Predictor)** |
| **H2: GPS $\rightarrow$ GBI** | **$0.204$** | **$5.361$** | **$p < 0.001$** | **Supported** |
| **H3: GPT $\rightarrow$ GBI** | **$0.122$** | **$2.694$** | **$p = 0.008$** | **Supported** |
| **H4: GPV $\rightarrow$ GBI** | **$0.137$** | **$3.712$** | **$p < 0.001$** | **Supported** |

#### 3. Moderating Role of Environmental Awareness:
* **GEA $\times$ GPS $\rightarrow$ GBI**: Statistically significant positive moderation ($\beta = 0.099, t = 2.031, p = 0.045$).
  - **Crucial Finding**: High Green Environmental Awareness **buffers and overcomes price sensitivity barriers**. When students possess deep awareness of environmental deterioration, their willingness to purchase green products remains strong despite higher price tags.

#### 4. Necessary Condition Analysis (NCA):
* NCA demonstrated that all four variables (GTP, GPS, GPT, GPV) are strictly **necessary conditions** for achieving high GBI (effect size $d > 0.30$).
* To achieve an $80\%$ level of green behavioral intention among students, a minimum threshold of **$60.4\%$ GTP, $60.1\%$ GPS, $47.5\%$ GPT, and $51.2\%$ GPV** must be met.

---

### Strategic Recommendations for Green Technology Advising
1. **Holistic TPB Engagement**: Focus on building positive attitudes, encouraging peer recommendations, and facilitating accessible purchasing channels.
2. **Cultivating Environmental Awareness to Diminish Price Barriers**: Because high environmental awareness softens student price resistance, advisory tools should educate users on long-term ecological and lifecycle cost benefits rather than upfront sticker price alone.
3. **Transparent Trust Signaling**: Transparently communicate certifications (e.g., EPEAT, Energy Star, RoHS) to validate product trust and eliminate consumer skepticism over greenwashing.
