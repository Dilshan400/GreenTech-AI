# Impact of Green Marketing Tools on Green Product Purchase Behavior: Electronic Home Appliances in Sri Lanka

**Authors**: J. P. N. Sewwandi & P. K. C. Dinesha (Department of Marketing, University of Ruhuna, Sri Lanka)  
**Publication**: *Asian Journal of Marketing Management (AJMM)*, 2022, Vol. 1, Issue 1, pp. 89–114.

---

### Research Purpose & Context
This study empirically examines how green marketing tools impact consumers' green purchasing behavior regarding electronic home appliances in Sri Lanka, focusing on the retail customer base in the Matara district (Singer Sri Lanka showrooms). It investigates the direct influence of three primary marketing tools and the moderating role of consumer demographic factors (gender and education level).

---

### Core Theoretical Framework
Grounding the inquiry in the **Social-Ecological Model** (Bourdieu, 1992; Lejano & Stokols, 2013) and behavior change communication paradigms, the research evaluates:
* **Independent Variables (Green Marketing Tools)**:
  1. **Environmental Advertisements (EA)**: Stimulating awareness of eco-friendly product features, less-consuming power sources, and sustainable manufacturing.
  2. **Perception of Eco-Labels (PEL)**: Informative markers that bridge information asymmetry regarding how electronic products are manufactured and certified.
  3. **Perception of Eco-Brands (PEB)**: Differentiating eco-friendly products as symbols of environmental reliability, credibility, and reduced ecological footprints.
* **Moderating Variables**: Consumer Gender and Education Level.
* **Dependent Variable**: Green Purchasing Behavior (GPB) in electronic home appliances.

---

### Key Empirical Findings
From a cross-sectional survey of 150 electronic appliance consumers ($54\%$ male, $46\%$ female; $42\%$ aged 26–35):

1. **Overall Explanatory Power**:
   - The regression model explained **$68.9\%$ of the variance** in green purchasing decisions ($R^2 = 0.689, F = 70.354, p < 0.001$).
2. **Impact of Specific Marketing Tools**:
   - **Environmental Advertising (EA)** was the most influential predictor ($\beta = 0.396, p = 0.000$), showing that proactive media campaigns strongly enhance consumer purchasing action in the Sri Lankan market.
   - **Perception of Eco-Brands (PEB)** demonstrated a significant positive effect ($\beta = 0.236, p = 0.003$).
   - **Perception of Eco-Labels (PEL)** demonstrated a significant positive effect ($\beta = 0.235, p = 0.002$).
3. **Demographic Moderation Effects (SPSS Process Macro)**:
   - **Gender**: Did not significantly moderate the relationships ($p > 0.05$ across EA, PEL, and PEB). Both male and female consumers responded similarly to green marketing tools.
   - **Education Level**: Significantly moderated the relationship between **Eco-Labeling and Green Purchasing Behavior** ($\beta = -0.1372, p = 0.0387$), showing that higher education levels alter how consumers scrutinize and react to eco-labels.

---

### Practical Implications for Green Electronics in Sri Lanka
- **Standardized Eco-Label System**: Sri Lankan policymakers and manufacturers need to establish a recognized, uniform national eco-label system backed by the government and the Central Environmental Authority (CEA).
- **Public Educational Advertising**: Marketers should leverage environmental advertisements to educate buyers on energy ratings, power-saving capabilities, and repairability rather than assuming consumers already understand technical eco-labels.
