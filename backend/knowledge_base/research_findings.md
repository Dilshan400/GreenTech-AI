# Research Insights: Green Purchase Intention among IT Undergraduates in Sri Lanka


## Key Factors Influencing Green Purchase Intention

Research indicates that green purchase intention (GPI) for electronic devices is driven by a combination of cognitive, social, and environmental factors:

1. **Environmental Concern (EC)**:
   - Refers to a student's emotional involvement and awareness of ecological issues (such as E-waste pollution and global warming).
   - While general environmental concern is high among Sri Lankan undergraduates, it does not always translate directly into a purchase because of barriers like cost and availability. This is known as the **"Green Attitude-Behavior Gap"**.

2. **Green Product Knowledge (GPK)**:
   - Measures how well students understand environmental labels (EPEAT, Energy Star), conflict-free manufacturing, and the lifecycle impact of devices.
   - IT students possess high technical literacy but often lack awareness of specific green certifications. Providing clear information about repairability scores and energy consumption directly increases their purchase intention.

3. **Social Influence (SI)**:
   - Peers, lecturers, and university culture play a strong role in shaping students' choices.
   - When departments promote eco-labeled products or set up recycling drives, students are more likely to prioritize sustainable electronics to conform to positive group norms.

4. **Perceived Price & Willingness to Pay (WTP)**:
   - Green electronics often carry a premium price tag.
   - Since most Sri Lankan state university undergraduates are budget-constrained, high prices act as a major deterrent. However, students show a higher willingness to pay a premium when they are shown the long-term energy savings (e.g., lower electricity bills) or modularity benefits (saving money by upgrading parts instead of buying a new laptop).

## Barriers to Sustainable Purchases in Sri Lankan Universities

* **Availability**: Limited local importers specialize in EPEAT Gold laptops, making them harder to find compared to standard models.
* **Information Asymmetry**: Retailers in Sri Lanka rarely highlight environmental specifications, forcing students to manually research them.
* **Economic Constraints**: The high initial cost of modular electronics makes refurbished corporate laptops a highly viable and recommended alternative for students.
