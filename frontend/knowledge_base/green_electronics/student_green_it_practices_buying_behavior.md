# Factors Influencing Green IT Practices, Device Purchasing, and Subscription Behaviors

**Authors**: Nelson K. Y. Leung, Sim Kim Lau, & Sim Yee Lau (Swinburne University of Technology, University of Wollongong, Reitaku University)  
**Publication**: *Pacific Asia Journal of the Association for Information Systems (PAJAIS)*, Vol. 11, Issue 1, pp. 88–107, 2019.

---

### Research Focus: Green IT at the Consumer Level
While extensive literature focuses on organizational Green IT (sourcing, operations, enterprise carbon footprint), this study evaluates Green IT from the perspective of **young consumers and university students** who heavily purchase and consume personal computing hardware, smartphones, and digital streaming services.

---

### Conceptual Framework & Hypotheses
Adapting the **Theory of Planned Behavior (TPB)** and Oom Do Valle et al.'s behavioral models:
* **Understanding of Green IT Practices (Specific Knowledge)**: Comprehension of sustainable hardware production, low-energy usage, and eco-friendly disposal.
* **Communication Strategy**: Exposure to public information (internet, television, news) and vendor-provided information.
* **Perceived Green IT Behavioral Control (PBC)**: Individual confidence, ability, and controllability in performing Green IT actions.
* **Outcomes (Green IT Behavior)**:
  1. Everyday Green IT Practices (e.g., energy management, double-sided printing).
  2. Buying Behavior of Computer Devices (PCs, laptops, tablets, smartphones).
  3. Subscription Behavior of Video and Music Streaming Services.

---

### Empirical Findings
Cross-national survey of **277 university students** across five countries (Hong Kong, USA, Japan, Vietnam, Australia):

#### 1. Structural Path Analysis:
* **PBC $\rightarrow$ Buying Behavior of Computer Devices**: Strong, highly significant positive relationship ($\beta = 0.82, p < 0.001$).
* **PBC $\rightarrow$ Subscription Behavior of Streaming Services**: Strong, highly significant positive relationship ($\beta = 0.88, p < 0.001$).
* **PBC $\rightarrow$ Day-to-Day Green IT Practices**: Significant positive relationship ($\beta = 0.32, p < 0.05$).
* **Knowledge $\rightarrow$ PBC**: Understanding of Green IT practices significantly enhances PBC ($\beta = 0.30, p < 0.05$).
* **Communication Strategy $\rightarrow$ PBC**: Directly and positively strengthens behavioral control ($\beta = 0.44, p < 0.001$).

#### 2. The Attitude-Behavior Reality in Hardware Purchasing:
When students were surveyed on factors influencing PC/Smartphone buying decisions:
* **Traditional Performance Factors Rank Highest**:
  - Processor speed: $88.1\%$
  - Battery life: $88.1\%$
  - Screen quality: $86.6\%$
  - Price: $86.3\%$
  - Build quality: $85.9\%$
  - RAM size: $82.3\%$
* **Green IT Attributes Rank Lowest**:
  - Non-toxic materials: $45.5\%$
  - Manufacturer operates in an energy-efficient manner: $39.7\%$
  - Manufacturer reuse/refurbish/take-back programs: $39.4\%$
  - Device components can be recycled: $35.7\%$
  - Manufacturer supplier green track record: $33.9\%$

#### 3. Low End-of-Life Engagement:
While students frequently carry out simple behavioral habits like double-sided printing ($70.8\%$) and turning off unused devices ($67.5\%$), **end-of-life hardware recycling was low**:
* Only $39.7\%$ dispose of IT equipment in an environmentally friendly manner.
* Only $39.4\%$ sell or donate unwanted IT equipment.
* Only $39.4\%$ recycle IT consumables like ink cartridges.

---

### Implications for Green Tech Advising
- **The Information Deficit**: Over $60\%$ of students noted that manufacturers and retailers fail to provide clear, accessible Green IT information on product labels or storefronts.
- **Actionable Advice for IT Buyers**: An AI advisory system must actively bridge this information gap by surfacing energy star ratings, modularity, repairability indexes, and trade-in/take-back opportunities alongside core technical specifications (RAM, CPU, price).
