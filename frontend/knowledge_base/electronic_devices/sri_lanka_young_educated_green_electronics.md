# Determinants of Green Electronic Devices Purchasing Decisions: Young, Educated Consumers in Sri Lanka

**Authors**: I. D. K. Ranganath (Lyceum International School) & G. P. K. Nishadi (Department of Marketing, University of Ruhuna, Sri Lanka)  
**Publication**: *Asian Journal of Marketing Management (AJMM)*, Vol. 1, Issue 2, 2022, pp. 51–69.

---

### Research Context & Rationale
While green purchasing behavior has been explored in general FMCG sectors, the determinants of **green electronic devices purchasing decisions** among young, educated consumers (undergraduates) in developing Asian economies like Sri Lanka have remained under-researched.

The study investigated demand and purchasing decisions for key green electronic devices:
* Eco-friendly laptops and computing hardware.
* Inverter and energy-saving air conditioners (A/C).
* CFC-free refrigerators.
* Energy-saving televisions and LED displays.
* Eco-friendly batteries and lighting.

---

### Conceptual Framework & Hypotheses
Tested via multiple regression analysis based on four primary determinants:
1. **Environmental Concern (EC)**: Emotional and rational engagement with environmental degradation in Sri Lanka.
2. **Supporting Environmental Protection (SEP)**: Belief that using green electronics is meaningful and aligns with personal lifestyle.
3. **Environmental Friendliness of Businesses (EFB)**: Preference for ethical electronics manufacturers and boycotting known polluters.
4. **Social Influence (SI)**: Peer communication, friend recommendations, and the social appeal of using eco-friendly devices.

---

### Empirical Findings
Conducted with **200 university students** across the Southern Province of Sri Lanka (Galle, Matara, Hambantota districts):

#### 1. High Explanatory Model Fit:
* The four independent variables explain **$78.2\%$ of the variance** in green electronic purchasing decisions ($R^2 = 0.782, \text{Adjusted } R^2 = 0.778, F = 174.945, p < 0.001$).
* Model equation:  
  $$\text{GPPD} = 0.143 + 0.351(\text{EC}) + 0.207(\text{SEP}) + 0.137(\text{EFB}) + 0.280(\text{SI})$$

#### 2. Relative Impact of Predictors:
| Predictor Variable | Standardized $\beta$ | $t$-statistic | $p$-value | Significance |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Environmental Concern (EC)** | **$0.362$** | **$6.362$** | **$0.000$** | **Strongest Predictor** |
| **Social Influence (SI)** | **$0.262$** | **$4.556$** | **$0.000$** | **Second Strongest** |
| **Supporting Environmental Protection (SEP)** | $0.215$ | $4.163$ | $0.000$ | Statistically Significant |
| **Environmental Friendliness of Businesses (EFB)** | $0.168$ | $3.683$ | $0.000$ | Statistically Significant |

---

### Strategic Takeaways for GreenTech Advisor AI
* **Undergraduates are Willing to Pay for Eco-Electronics**: Young educated consumers in Sri Lanka recognize that paying a premium is worthwhile for electronic devices that tangibly conserve energy and reduce toxic waste.
* **Information Alignment**: Electronics retailers and advisory systems should link technical specs (processor speeds, displays, battery life) with concrete environmental benefits (energy star ratings, non-toxic RoHS solder, recyclable chassis) to satisfy both functional and ecological customer demands.
