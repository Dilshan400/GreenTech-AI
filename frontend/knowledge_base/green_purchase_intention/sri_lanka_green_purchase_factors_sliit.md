# Factors Influencing Green Purchase Intentions of Consumers Across Sri Lanka

**Authors**: W. D. N. Madhavika, D. I. Amaraweera, M. S. A. Fernando, K. K. D. H. Perera, & M. N. D. Perera (SLIIT Business School, Sri Lanka)  
**Publication**: *SLIIT Business Review*, Vol. 1, No. 2, 2021, pp. 1–24.

---

### Research Context & Population Scope
This nationwide empirical study investigates the key determinants influencing consumers' green purchase intentions across **all 25 administrative districts in Sri Lanka**. Using probability cluster sampling, data were collected from a representative sample of **384 adult consumers** ($70.3\%$ aged 19–30; $52.3\%$ female, $47.7\%$ male).

---

### Variables & Regression Framework
Grounded in **Environmental Citizenship Theory** (Dobson, 2003), the study analyzes six independent variables:
* **Awareness of Eco-Friendly Offerings (A)**
* **Environmental Concern (EC)**
* **Green Advertising (GA)**
* **Greenwashing (GW)**
* **Product Attributes (PA)** (e.g., eco-labeling, durability, energy efficiency, non-toxic sourcing)
* **Social Norms (SN)**

Multiple linear regression formula:
$$Y = \beta_0 + \beta_1(A) + \beta_2(EC) + \beta_3(GA) + \beta_4(GW) + \beta_5(PA) + \beta_6(SN) + \epsilon$$

---

### Empirical Findings

#### 1. Explanatory Power:
* The model accounts for **$59.6\%$ of the variance in green purchase intention** ($R^2 = 0.596, \text{Adjusted } R^2 = 0.590, F = 92.701, p < 0.001$).

#### 2. Regression Results & Significance:
| Variable | Unstandardized Coeff. ($B$) | Std. Error | $p$-value | Decision |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Green Advertising (GA)** | **$0.335$** | $0.064$ | **$p = 0.000$** | **Supported (Most Influential)** |
| **Environmental Concern (EC)** | **$0.220$** | $0.065$ | **$p = 0.001$** | **Supported** |
| **Product Attributes (PA)** | **$0.150$** | $0.070$ | **$p = 0.032$** | **Supported** |
| **Awareness (A)** | **$0.125$** | $0.051$ | **$p = 0.016$** | **Supported** |
| Social Norms (SN) | $0.098$ | $0.056$ | $p = 0.079$ | Insignificant |
| Greenwashing (GW) | $0.018$ | $0.057$ | $p = 0.755$ | Insignificant |

---

### Practical Implications
1. **Green Advertising as Primary Catalyst**: Authentic advertising that clearly demonstrates genuine ecological benefits has the highest predictive power on Sri Lankan consumer purchase decisions ($B = 0.335$).
2. **Product Attributes Matter More Than Abstract Social Pressure**: Consumers prioritize tangible product features (certifications, packaging, energy ratings) over social peer pressure when committing financial resources to eco-friendly options.
3. **Overcoming Information Gaps**: Sri Lankan consumers are increasingly receptive to green products, but businesses must proactively communicate product attributes through clear, verified educational advertising.
