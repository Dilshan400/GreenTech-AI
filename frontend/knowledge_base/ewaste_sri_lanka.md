# Electronic Waste (E-Waste) Management in Sri Lanka

Electronic waste (E-waste) is one of the fastest-growing waste streams in Sri Lanka. It comprises discarded laptops, smartphones, chargers, cathode-ray tubes (CRTs), printed circuit boards, and household appliances. Improper disposal leads to toxic heavy metals (lead, cadmium, mercury) leaching into the soil and water supply.

## Regulatory Framework: Central Environmental Authority (CEA)

In Sri Lanka, E-waste is categorized as **Hazardous Waste** under the National Environmental Act (NEA).
* Under the **National Environmental (Hazardous Waste Management) Regulations**, a license is required to collect, transport, store, recover, or recycle E-waste.
* Burning or dumping E-waste in municipal trash binds toxic chemical gases like dioxins into the atmosphere and contaminates local landfills.

## Designated E-Waste Collection & Recycling Centers in Sri Lanka

Sri Lankan consumers should never discard e-waste with general household garbage. Instead, use designated routes:

1. **State-licensed E-Waste Recyclers**:
   - Several private partners are licensed by the CEA for safe, high-tech electronic recycling. They extract valuable metals (copper, gold, silver) in controlled facilities and safely neutralize hazardous materials.
   
2. **Corporate Take-Back Programs**:
   - Leading telecommunications providers in Sri Lanka (e.g., Dialog Axiata, Mobitel) run periodic E-waste drop-off campaigns. Drop-off bins are placed at selected customer service centers for mobile phones, chargers, and small accessories.
   
3. **University and Institutional Drives**:
   - State universities frequently organize E-waste collection weeks in coordination with the CEA. IT undergraduates can drop off expired batteries, keyboards, and motherboard components at these events.

## Best Practices for Consumers

* **Separate Batteries**: Discard lithium-ion laptop/phone batteries separately. If a battery is swollen or leaking, handle it with gloves and store it in a cool, dry, non-metallic container before bringing it to a licensed collector.
* **Data Sanitization**: Before dropping off any storage device (hard drives, SSDs, smartphones) for recycling or donation, run a secure data wipe tool (e.g., DBAN or factory reset with encryption enabled) to protect personal data.
* **Donate/Reuse**: If a device is still functional but slow, consider donating it to local schools or community centers, or installing lightweight open-source operating systems (e.g., Lubuntu Linux) to extend its operating life.
